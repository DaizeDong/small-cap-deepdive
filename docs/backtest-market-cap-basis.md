# Historical market-cap evidence

`mktcap_asof()` produces a snapshot estimate from a dated quote and the latest
instantaneous common-share count disclosed by the requested date. Its `usable`
field controls whether that estimate can feed valuation. The complete evidence,
its status and its reason are retained in each backtest row.

Returns continue to use dividend-adjusted prices. Market-cap reconstruction uses
a separate Yahoo history request with `auto_adjust=False`, `back_adjust=False`
and `actions=True`. Yahoo's `Close` is still split-adjusted. The request therefore
covers the share observation through the current observation date and requires
an explicit `Stock Splits` column. A missing column cannot mean zero splits.
If the current observation date itself carries a split, the default provider
does not infer that the adjustment has finished; the estimate stays unresolved.
The share count is multiplied by the intervening new-shares-per-old-share ratios
so it has the same basis as the price. Cash dividends never multiply shares.

For example, a synthetic company has one million disclosed shares and a
historical price of $100. After a four-for-one split, Yahoo's historical Close
can be $25. The reconciled inputs are $25 and four million shares, preserving
the $100 million estimate. Multiplying $25 by the original one million shares
would understate the estimate by a factor of four.
These synthetic values are generated by `market_cap_basis_scenarios()` in
`tools/make_fixtures.py`.

The default share resolver uses only `dei:EntityCommonStockSharesOutstanding`
and `us-gaap:CommonStockSharesOutstanding`. It reads raw SEC responses through
`instant_share_evidence()` and verifies the issuer, concept and `shares` unit
before selection. It chooses the latest eligible observation, then its latest
filing. Different counts at that same observation and filing are a conflict;
identical duplicates agree, and a later eligible filing can revise an earlier
one. Disclosures after the requested date cannot replace available facts.
Weighted-average diluted shares do not substitute for an instantaneous count.
If a split occurred between the count's observation and filing, its restatement
basis is uncertain and the estimate remains unresolved.

The `shares_lookup` envelope retains every query's status, HTTP status, units
and diagnostic, along with the selected evidence and the continuation policy.
A successful empty response or HTTP 404 can establish absence. HTTP 429, other
unsuccessful statuses and transport errors remain provider errors; malformed
JSON, mismatched identity, unsupported units and invalid facts remain invalid
evidence. The default policy, `require_all_concept_queries_resolved`, allows
valuation only when every query returned valid evidence or confirmed absence.
An error in one concept therefore blocks using a count from the other concept.
The full envelope remains in the backtest row, including when a later price
lookup fails. The generic financial-series helper keeps its existing behavior.

Callbacks for an alternative source can return structured evidence:

- A price record contains `price`, `date`, `basis` (`as_traded` or
  `split_adjusted`) and `basis_date`. Split-adjusted records require an explicit
  basis date. Dividend-adjusted or undeclared bases cannot qualify.
- A share record contains `shares`, `date`, `filed`, `basis` (`as_traded`,
  `as_reported` or `split_adjusted`) and `basis_date`. `date` is the observation
  date, not a guessed filing date. Its observation cannot be later than the
  quote; the filing cannot be later than the requested date.
- When the two bases differ, the price record also contains `actions` with
  inclusive `start` and `end`, `complete: true`, and a `splits` list of
  `{date, ratio}` records. Coverage must span both bases. A conflicting duplicate,
  invalid ratio or incomplete interval leaves the result unresolved. A source
  using a split's effective date as a basis must specify
  `basis_timing: after_actions`; dates alone cannot resolve that ambiguity.

`price`, normalized `shares`, `reported_shares`, both basis dates, filing date,
action coverage and the applied `split_factor` accompany a compatible estimate.
The result remains a **latest disclosed count estimate**. It does not infer
issuance or repurchases after the count, prove an executable quote, resolve ADR
ratios or establish that an entity-wide count matches a particular share class.
Those are separate source-selection obligations. Conflicting supplied currency
or share-class identifiers are rejected.

Legacy `(price, date)` and scalar-share callbacks retain their arithmetic output
to support older callers and tests. They explicitly return
`basis_status: legacy_unverified` and `usable: false`. The production backtest
retains that evidence, uses no market cap for valuation and prevents a BUY
classification. Other unresolved or invalid results retain `mktcap: null`, their
reason and the same qualification restriction. No current `marketCap`, adjusted
price or zero value is silently promoted to a valid historical market cap.
